\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:methodology}

\hspace*{\parindent}This chapter discusses the research and development approach adopted in building the proposed system. It outlines the overall design framework, the mapping of objectives to specific methods, and the chosen software development life cycle model. The phases and activities of the development process are detailed, along with the detailed system design. By presenting the methodology, this chapter establishes how the study systematically progresses from planning to implementation, ensuring that the system meets both functional requirements and quality standards.
\vspace{5mm}

\section{Systems Analysis}
\label{sec:systems_analysis}

\subsection{Research and Development Approach}
\label{subsec:research_dev_approach}

\hspace*{\parindent}This study follows a research and development (R\&D) approach, in which the KABISIG system was analyzed, designed, developed, tested, and evaluated with SK stakeholders in Naga City. The objectives of the study are mapped to the methods as follows. Objective 1 (identify constraints of the existing system) was addressed through interviews, document analysis, and observation (Section~\ref{subsubsec:requirements_gathering}). Objective 2 (develop a standardized, cloud-based, multi-tenant system) was addressed through Agile Scrum development (Sections~\ref{subsec:software_dev_methodology} and~\ref{subsec:phases_and_activities}). Objective 3 (design and develop the five core modules) was addressed through the functional requirements and sprint plan (Sections 4.2.17.1 and 4.2.17.2). Objective 4 (evaluate satisfaction and usability) was addressed through the ISO/IEC 25010 and TAM evaluations (Sections 4.2.18.2 and 4.2.18.3). Objective 5 (evaluate performance) was addressed through functional accuracy checks and performance testing (Section 4.2.18.2).
\vspace{5mm}

\subsection{Overall Design}
\label{subsec:overall_design}

\hspace*{\parindent}This project utilizes Agile-inspired approaches of continuous development and review, specifically adopting the Scrum framework. The iterative development approach is used whereby the capabilities of the system are improved iteratively through development, testing, and feedback loops. This approach ensures that the system developed is aligned with user expectations and meets the technical specifications.
\vspace{5mm}

\subsection{Staged Deployment Across Naga City}
\label{subsec:phased_deployment}

\hspace*{\parindent}The project follows a staged approach for deployment across Naga City:
\vspace{5mm}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Stage 1 (Foundation):} Multi-tenant system with pre-provisioned tenants, authentication, user management, youth registration and verification, program management, and calendar view, in three pilot barangays.
    \item \textbf{Stage 2 (Operations):} QR attendance tracking, expense tracking, budget allocation, simple report generation, and in-app notifications, in three barangays.
    \item \textbf{Stage 3 (Engagement):} Boses ng Kabataan feedback, resolution tracking with youth polling, document management with approval routing, compliance monitoring, and basic public viewing, in five barangays.
    \item \textbf{Stage 4 (Scale):} Barangay dashboards, federation analytics, PDF report generation, the Public Transparency Dashboard, and Facebook Page integration, across all barangays of Naga City that have an assigned SK Chairperson.
    \item \textbf{Stage 5 (Optimize):} Performance optimization, bug fixing, administrator documentation, and user training materials.
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{5mm}

\section{Systems Design}
\label{sec:systems_design}

\subsection{Software Development Methodology}
\label{subsec:software_dev_methodology}

\hspace*{\parindent}The proposed project uses the Agile software development life cycle, specifically Scrum, because the requirements of the SK councils were not fully known at the start and changed as stakeholders tried the system. Agile lets the researchers build the system in small parts called sprints, test each module before joining it with the others, and fix problems early based on feedback. Development followed two-week sprints. The requirements gathered through interviews, document analysis, and observation formed the list of tasks to build. Each sprint included planning, regular standups, a review of the finished work with stakeholders, and a retrospective. Modules were built in order of dependency: authentication, tenant setup, and roles first, then youth registration, programs and attendance, budget and expenses, documents, compliance, and feedback, and finally analytics, the public dashboard, and Facebook integration. After development, the system was evaluated using the TAM and ISO/IEC 25010 questionnaires.
\vspace{5mm}

\subsection{Phases and Activities}
\label{subsec:phases_and_activities}

\hspace*{\parindent}The development process follows the Agile life cycle. Planning, design, development, and testing are repeated in each sprint, and deployment and maintenance follow the final increment.
\vspace{5mm}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Planning:} System requirements were defined through consultations with SK stakeholders and recommendations from the panel. Requirements were prioritized using the MoSCoW technique (Must have, Should have, Could have, Won't have).
    \item \textbf{Design:} A multitenant architecture was designed in which each barangay's records are isolated by \texttt{tenant\_id}, with role-based access control and audit logging. Interfaces were designed for each of the seven roles: Super Admin, SK Chairperson, Kagawad, Secretary, Treasurer, Youth, and Public Viewer.
    \item \textbf{Development:} The system was built iteratively in order of functional dependency: authentication, tenant setup, and roles; youth registration and verification; programs and QR attendance; budget, expenses, and inventory; documents, resolutions, and compliance monitoring; feedback, polls, and sentiment analysis; notifications, announcements, and Facebook integration; and analytics and the public transparency dashboard. The interface was made responsive across devices.
    \item \textbf{Testing:} Each module underwent functional testing before integration. The completed system was evaluated through performance checks, user testing with the TAM questionnaire, and an ISO/IEC 25010 evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Deployment:} The system is deployed on Vercel for web access across devices.
    \item \textbf{Maintenance:} Planned maintenance activities include bug fixing, security patches, feature enhancements, and user support after turnover to the SK.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Requirements Gathering}
\label{subsubsec:requirements_gathering}

\hspace*{\parindent}Requirements were collated through:
\begin{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Account Creation and Approval Workflow}
\label{subsubsec:account_creation_approval_workflow}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Super Administrator (SK Federation President)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Account Provisioning:} The account is pre-made directly by the system developer during initial database deployment and does not pass through any administrative approval queue.
        \item \textit{Login \& Onboarding Workflow:} Uses the ``Forgot Password'' feature on the login screen to establish a permanent master password and complete account initialization, and signs in via the Secure Sign-In Portal using the pre-configured administrative email and newly created password.
        \item \textit{Access:} Oversees all 27 barangays of Naga City and can view analytics and data across all of them, but cannot directly manage the day-to-day youth data of a specific barangay.
        \item \textit{Operational Workflow:} Navigates to the Barangay Management module, selects a pre-created barangay from the 27 barangays, assigns the official email address of the appointed SK Chairperson or SK Officials (Kagawad, Secretary, and Treasurer), and sends an automated system invitation link. Handles resignation, end of term, or succession by demoting the outgoing SK Chairperson and promoting the new one via the transfer leadership feature.
        \item \textit{Federation Analytics \& Audit Oversight:} Views aggregated city-wide data (budget utilization, demographics, and program participation) on the Federation Dashboard and views audit logs to track actions across all roles.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Barangay Administrator (SK Chairperson)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Account Provisioning:} Created via an automated invitation link sent by the Super Administrator to a pre-created barangay without undergoing an administrative approval queue, as creation/invitation by the Super Admin acts as direct authorization.
        \item \textit{Login \& Onboarding Workflow:} Receives the system invitation link and clicks it to open the credential setup page, enters their full legal name and sets an account password, submits the form to set the account status directly to active, and logs into the Barangay Administrator interface via the Sign-In Portal.
        \item \textit{Access:} Restricted to their own barangay (\texttt{tenant\_id}).
        \item \textit{Operational Workflow:} Opens the Pending Registrations module, reviews pending youth registrations and uploaded IDs, and approves or rejects each one. Sends invitation links to the SK Kagawad, SK Secretary, and SK Treasurer of the barangay. Sets the total SK annual budget in accordance with DILG JMC No. 1, s. 2025. Reviews and approves or rejects the resolutions, minutes, and liquidation reports submitted by the Secretary. Views the programs created by the Kagawad, oversees the inventory, and configures the official SK Facebook Page integration via the Meta Graph API to publish community announcements.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{SK Officials (SK Kagawad, SK Secretary, SK Treasurer)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Account Provisioning \& Onboarding:} Receives an automated invitation link (sent by the Super Administrator or SK Chairperson) that specifies the council position (SK Kagawad, SK Secretary, or SK Treasurer) for an already created barangay, clicks the link to go to the credential setup page, enters their full name and sets an account password, submits the form to activate the account and link it to the barangay (with no approval queue involved, since the invitation acts as authorization), and logs in through the Sign-In page using their KABISIG email and password.
        \item \textit{Access:} Restricted to their own barangay (\texttt{tenant\_id}). All three positions share the same role in the database (the SK Official role), and the interface routes each official to different functions according to their position.
        \item \textit{Operational Workflows:}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textit{SK Kagawad (Program Officer):} Creates programs and schedules events, monitors participant slot quotas, uses the QR scanner in the Attendance Tracking module to scan attendees' Digital Youth ID QR codes, and reviews Boses ng Kabataan feedback and sentiment analysis results.
            \item \textit{SK Secretary:} Uploads documents and drafts resolutions in the Document Repository, routes documents to the SK Chairperson for approval, tracks their approval status, tracks compliance deadlines, and records official council attendance during regular meetings and administrative sessions.
            \item \textit{SK Treasurer:} Allocates program budgets from the total SK annual budget set by the SK Chairperson, records itemized expenses with automated VAT and non-VAT calculations, tracks remaining balances, and manages the Inventory Registry to track SK properties and assets, log equipment conditions, and generate turnover reports.
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Youth Constituent (Resident, Aged 15 to 30)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Account Provisioning \& Onboarding:} Registers through the public portal and selects the barangay (available only if an SK Chairperson is assigned to that barangay), completes the profile form, uploads an ID, and passes system validation confirming the birthdate shows an age of 15 to 30 and the address is within the barangay. Enters a pending status upon submission while waiting for the SK Chairperson's approval; upon approval, account status switches to active and the youth receives a Digital Youth ID with a unique QR code (on rejection, the youth is notified of the reason and registers again). Logs in through the Sign-In page with their KABISIG email and password.
        \item \textit{Access:} Can view only their own profile, the public programs, and the transparency portal.
        \item \textit{Operational Workflow:} Browses public programs and registers for them (registration is blocked if the verification status is not verified), presents the Digital Youth ID QR code to SK officials for attendance scanning, submits feedback (optionally anonymous, which sets \texttt{user\_id} to \texttt{NULL}) through Boses ng Kabataan, and casts votes in official community surveys and youth polls.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Public Viewer (Unauthenticated Citizen)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Account Provisioning:} Requires no account creation, no invitation, and no login credentials, remaining open to the general public to maintain compliance with the DILG Full Public Disclosure policy.
        \item \textit{Access \& Operational Workflow:} Navigates directly to the public KABISIG web address through any standard browser to access read-only public pages, viewing the Public Transparency Dashboard including budget summaries, program accomplishments, and announcements.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Functional Requirements}
\label{subsec:functional_requirements}

\subsubsection{System Features}
\label{subsubsec:system_features}

\hspace*{\parindent}The KABISIG system is a web-based decision support information system for inclusive governance built on a multi-tenant architecture covering all 27 barangays of Naga City. Rather than a basic CRUD application, it integrates five rule-based operational modules that directly support the legal mandates and daily governance processes of the Sangguniang Kabataan.

Each module below is described in terms of its implemented capabilities, mapped to the role(s) responsible for it, consistent with the system's Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) model: Super Admin (SK Federation President), Barangay Admin (SK Chairperson), SK Official (Kagawad, Secretary, Treasurer), Youth Constituent, and Public Viewer.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MODULE 1: Kabataan Profiling \& Digital Registry (Core Module)}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Primary Roles:} Youth Constituent (registration), Barangay Admin (verification), Super Admin (cross-barangay oversight)
        \item \textit{Resident Registration:} Provides a public online portal for youth registration (ages 15--30). New registrants enter a pending state and are visible only within their own barangay tenant until the SK Chairperson verifies their uploaded ID. The system enforces age validation (15--30) at the account level. Multi-parameter search and filtering (name, email, zone/purok, educational institution) is scoped to the SK Chairperson's own \texttt{tenant\_id}.
        \item \textit{Digital Registry:} Maintains comprehensive youth demographic profiles --- zone/purok classification, educational tracking, employment profiling, beneficiary categorization, scholar status, and youth sector tagging --- aligned with the Katipunan ng Kabataan profiling mandate (DILG MC No. 2022-033). Data isolation is enforced via tenant-scoped row-level access; the Super Admin sees only aggregated analytics, never individual youth records of a specific barangay.
        \item \textit{Digital Identification:} Automatically issues a standardized Digital Youth ID with a unique auto-generated identifier and embedded QR code upon SK Chairperson approval --- not upon registration --- ensuring only verified constituents hold valid digital IDs.
        \item \textit{Participation \& Beneficiary Monitoring:} Tracks cumulative participation histories, received assistance, and attendance records, with duplicate assistance detection to support equitable resource distribution.
        \item \textit{Rule-Based Demographic Classification:} Deterministically assigns constituents to categories using if-then logic: Student, Out-of-School Youth (OSY), Scholar, Person with Disability (PWD), Solo Parent, Employed, and Unemployed.
        \item \textit{Youth Engagement Scoring:} Computes a rule-based engagement score (0--100) from attendance frequency, committee leadership, and completed programs, banded as: 90--100 Highly Active, 70--89 Active, 50--69 Moderately Active, and Below 50 Needs Engagement (flagged for targeted outreach).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{MODULE 2: Program, Event \& Rule-Based Recommendation Management}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Primary Roles:} Kagawad (Program Officer) creates and runs programs; Barangay Admin provides oversight; Youth Constituent registers
        \item \textit{Program Lifecycle Management:} Supports program creation, event scheduling, integrated calendar management, categorization, and progress monitoring aligned with the ABYIP. Programs are created by the Kagawad and supervised (read/managed) by the SK Chairperson.
        \item \textit{Attendance \& Participation Tracking:} Uses camera-based QR scanning (\texttt{getUserMedia} + \texttt{html5-qrcode}) to record arrivals, prevent duplicate check-ins, and log historical attendance.
        \item \textit{Rule-Based Program Recommendations:} Generates targeted activity suggestions from profile attributes (age, education, school, employment, scholar status, beneficiary category, past interests, engagement score) using if-then rules.
        \item \textit{Registration Gating:} Program registration is strictly blocked for youth whose \texttt{verification\_status} is not verified, reinforcing the registry-first integrity of the system.
        \item \textit{Low Engagement Detection:} Flags residents below active engagement thresholds for inclusive outreach by SK officials.
        \item \textit{Rule-Based Schedule Conflict Detection:} Cross-references dates, timeframes, lead officials, and venues to flag collisions before event confirmation.
    \end{itemize}

   \item \textbf{MODULE 3: Financial Transparency \& Governance Support}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Primary Roles:} Barangay Admin (budget authorization), Treasurer (allocation, expenses, inventory), Secretary (documents), Public Viewer (transparency dashboard)
        \item \textit{Budget \& Expense Monitoring:} The SK Chairperson sets the total annual SK budget per DILG JMC No. 1, s. 2025; the Treasurer allocates amounts to programs and records itemized expenditures against those allocations, with utilization monitoring and liquidation receipts.
        \item \textit{Automated Financial Computations:} Automatic line-item balances, utilization percentages, and applicable tax deductions (VAT/non-VAT classifications).
        \item \textit{Governance Document Repository:} The Secretary uploads and drafts resolutions, ordinances, minutes, financial reports (including vouchers and liquidation reports), and project proposals; documents route to the SK Chairperson for electronic review and role-based sign-off.
        \item \textit{Automated Accomplishment Reporting:} Auto-generates standardized PDF accomplishment summaries and financial reports from system data.
        \item \textit{Compliance Monitoring:} Tracks required reports and their statuses (Pending, In Progress, Completed, Overdue) and surfaces compliance records and required signatories for manual officer review. The monitor tracks task completion status and does not automatically verify the completeness of uploaded files.
        \item \textit{Public Transparency Dashboard:} Discloses program fund allocations, utilization rates, completed activities, and public summaries without exposing private constituent data, fulfilling the DILG SK Full Public Disclosure (FPD) mandate.
    \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{MODULE 4: Community Engagement, Communication \& Rule-Based Feedback Analytics}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Primary Roles:} Youth Constituent (submission), Kagawad (review \& moderation), Barangay Admin (account restriction), all roles (notifications)
        \item \textit{Civic Engagement Portal (Boses ng Kabataan):} Registered youth submit suggestions, complaints, activity evaluations, and survey/poll responses.
        \item \textit{Anonymous Feedback Support:} An anonymity toggle allows sensitive concerns to be submitted without exposing identity (setting \texttt{user\_id} to \texttt{NULL} upon submission).
        \item \textit{Voting and Anonymity:} Only Boses ng Kabataan feedback submissions can be anonymous. Poll responses and resolution votes are linked to verified user accounts to prevent duplicate voting.
        \item \textit{Spam Mitigation, Auto-Flagging \& Account Moderation:} Automatically detects spam submissions and profanity/bad words in Boses ng Kabataan feedback, extracting and flagging qualifying entries as pending review by the Kagawad. For repeat or severe violations, administrators can enforce account-level sanctions via the \texttt{account\_restrictions} table (\texttt{temp\_suspend} or \texttt{permanent\_ban}) with full review history tracking.
        \item \textit{Two-Way Communication Channels:} In-app notifications deliver advisories, registration progress updates, calendar reminders, and system alerts.
        \item \textit{Rule-Based Feedback Analysis:} Replaces manual sentiment tagging with keyword-dictionary matching:
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \textbf{Sentiment Classification} as Positive, Negative, or Neutral.
            \item \textbf{Keyword Extraction \& Program Request Detection} for priorities such as Scholarship, Sports, Employment, Livelihood, and Mental Health.
            \item \textbf{Trend Analysis \& Reporting} via dashboards showing satisfaction indices, common concerns, and sentiment shifts to guide legislative and executive action.
        \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{MODULE 5: Analytics, Reporting \& Public Information}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Primary Roles:} Barangay Admin (barangay dashboards), Super Admin (federation analytics), Public Viewer (public portal)
        \item \textit{Interactive Operational Dashboards:} Live metrics on youth demographics, zone/purok distribution, program registrations, QR attendance, beneficiary counts, and budget utilization.
        \item \textit{Automated Report Generation:} Exports youth resident lists, attendance sheets, accomplishment briefs, demographic profiles, and financial utilization statements.
        \item \textit{Cross-Barangay Federation Analytics:} The Super Admin (SK Federation President) receives aggregated, city-wide comparisons across all 27 barangays --- including budget utilization, demographics, and program participation --- via Recharts dashboards. Critically, the Super Admin cannot directly manage day-to-day youth data of any specific barangay; tenant isolation is preserved and only aggregated views are surfaced.
        \item \textit{Audit Oversight:} The Super Admin can view the \texttt{audit\_logs} table to track actions across all roles, supporting accountability and the leadership transfer process (resignation, end of term, and succession handling through demotion of the outgoing SK Chairperson and promotion of the successor).
        \item \textit{Public Information Portal:} Hosts public-facing statistics, verified accomplishment summaries, and announcements accessible to unauthenticated citizens in read-only mode.
        \item \textit{Social Media Synchronization via Meta Graph API:} A one-way publishing pipeline that posts only approved official announcements from KABISIG to the barangay SK's official Facebook Page, using encrypted Page Access Tokens configured per barangay tenant.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Testing and Evaluation Procedures}
\label{subsubsec:testing_and_evaluation_procedures}

\paragraph{Types of Testing}
\label{par:types_of_testing}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Functional Testing:} Functional test cases verified each system module against core features: create, read, update, and delete (CRUD) operations; role-based access control (RBAC) and multi-tenant data isolation; youth verification and Digital Youth ID issuance; program registration gating; QR code attendance tracking; document approval routing (Draft, Pending Approval, Approved, Rejected); in-app notifications; compliance tracking; public dashboard filtering; and the mathematical accuracy of budget allocations, VAT/non-VAT tax deductions, and remaining balance computations. Each test case was recorded as a pass or fail, and the overall pass rate was reported. This testing directly addressed the functional suitability characteristic of the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 quality model.
    \item \textbf{Usability Testing:} Representative SK officials and youth constituents performed assigned tasks---such as account registration, program creation, program registration, feedback submission through Boses ng Kabataan, and document uploading---using the responsive web interface across desktop and mobile browsers. Task success rates were recorded. The Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) questionnaire was subsequently administered to measure perceived usefulness (PU), perceived ease of use (PEOU), attitude toward use (ATU), and behavioral intention to use (BIU).
    \item \textbf{User Acceptance Testing (UAT):} UAT was conducted with the SK Chairperson, operational SK officials (Kagawad, Secretary, Treasurer), and youth constituents to confirm that primary workflows (youth verification, QR attendance tracking, budget monitoring, document approval routing, and Boses ng Kabataan feedback) effectively met their operational requirements in a barangay setting prior to deployment. Stakeholders documented their formal acceptance and qualitative feedback for each workflow.
    \item \textbf{Performance Testing:} The system was evaluated for page load times (targeting under 2 seconds), REST API response latency (targeting under 500 ms), and PDF accomplishment report generation duration using execution logs and testing tools. Notification dispatch speed was also planned as a metric but was not separately benchmarked (see Section~\ref{subsec:performance_and_cross_browser_compatibility_testing}). QR camera scan recognition time was measured as a supplementary metric, and its measurement procedure is described in the same section. Load and stress testing were outside the scope of this evaluation; the planned approach is described in Section~\ref{subsubsec:maintenance_and_future_enhancements}. These empirical measurements complemented the performance efficiency ratings assessed during the ISO/IEC 25010:2023 expert evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Security Testing:} Security evaluations verified server-side payload validation, role-based access control permissions, multi-tenant row-level security (RLS) query isolation, access authorization checks for uploaded documents and IDs, and the comprehensive audit logging of sensitive administrative actions. This testing was guided by the security characteristic of ISO/IEC 25010:2023, system authentication standards via Supabase Auth, and compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Browser and Mobile Compatibility Testing:} Functional correctness, layout visual consistency, and responsive adaptability were verified across Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge, and Apple Safari for desktop environments, as well as iOS Safari and Android Chrome for mobile devices. The system functioned as a fully responsive web application accessible via modern mobile browsers without requiring a native mobile app installation.
    \item \textbf{ISO/IEC 25010:2023 Expert Evaluation:} Independent IT instructors, software developers, and LGU technical personnel evaluated the system across eight of the nine ISO/IEC 25010:2023 product quality characteristics (excluding safety, which was not applicable to this system) using a structured evaluation instrument.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Sampling Plan}
\label{subsubsec:sampling_plan}

To obtain a structured and relevant evaluation of the KABISIG system, purposive sampling was used to select SK officials and technical/IT evaluators, while stratified purposive sampling was used to select youth constituents across demographic groups, alongside convenience/purposive sampling for public viewers. The evaluation covered four respondent groups. Technical product quality was assessed using an ISO/IEC 25010:2023-based instrument answered by IT evaluators, while perceived usefulness, ease of use, and workflow suitability were evaluated through the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM) questionnaire alongside User Acceptance Testing (UAT) with SK officials, youth constituents, and public viewers.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{IT Experts / Technical Evaluators:} 3 to 5 IT instructors, software developers, or technical personnel who were not involved in developing KABISIG. They evaluated the system across eight ISO/IEC 25010:2023 product quality characteristics (all except safety).
    \item \textbf{SK Officials:} 30 active SK officials (including SK Kagawads and other available active council members across the 22 active barangays in Naga City, accounting for non-active barangays and councils with incomplete official lineups). Participants tested workflows relevant to their council operations---such as youth verification, budget and expense tracking, document routing, program management, and QR code attendance scanning---and completed the TAM questionnaire.
    \item \textbf{Youth Constituents:} 30 registered youth aged 15 to 30, stratified across three demographic groups (10 students, 10 out-of-school youth, and 10 working/employed youth). They tested public portal registration, program registration, Digital Youth ID usage, and the \textit{Boses ng Kabataan} feedback module, followed by the TAM questionnaire.
    \item \textbf{Public Viewers:} 10 unauthenticated citizens or community members who accessed the read-only Public Transparency Dashboard to view approved budget summaries, program accomplishments, and public announcements without logging in, followed by the Public Viewer TAM questionnaire.
    \item \textbf{Sample Size:} A total of 73 to 75 respondents (3--5 IT experts, 30 SK officials, 30 youth constituents, and 10 public viewers) was targeted for the evaluation. These sample sizes reflected actual barangay participation (22 of 27 barangays), council roster completeness, deployment timeline constraints, and practical academic evaluation standards. Results were reported per respondent group, and limitations arising from sample size and incomplete council participation were explicitly noted in the study's limitations. The number of respondents actually obtained and the reasons for the shortfall are reported in Section~\ref{sec:respondent_profile_and_turnout}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Maintenance and Future Enhancements}
\label{subsubsec:maintenance_and_future_enhancements}

\textbf{Maintenance:} Following system turnover to the Sangguniang Kabataan upon project completion, planned maintenance activities will include continuous bug fixing, security patch updates, and ongoing user support. System maintenance and technical support will be jointly managed by the researchers during the initial transition period, with long-term operational stewardship formally transferred to the respective barangay SK councils, the SK Federation of Naga City, and the City ICT Office.

\vspace{2mm}
\textbf{Future Enhancements:} The system can be extended in the following ways to further support local digital governance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Implementation of a dedicated scholar tracking module to monitor academic progress, semester-by-semester grades, and educational milestones, verifying whether enrolled student scholars have successfully graduated.
    \item Integration of an SMS notification gateway and email notifications for events and reminders, complementing the current in-app notifications and invitation emails.
    \item Implementation of hard spending limits and mandatory justification text capture for over-budget allocations and expense entries, replacing the current warning-only mechanism.
    \item Enabling the direct resubmission and updating of rejected youth registrations without requiring constituents to create a new registration record.
    \item Establishing automatic data linking between recorded itemized expenses and corresponding inventory asset entries.
    \item Evaluation of a standalone mobile application for QR attendance, as an alternative or complement to an installable progressive web application (PWA), for events with unstable internet connectivity. The proposed offline design is as follows. (a) Before the event, the authorized Kagawad's device downloads the program's roster of verified, registered youth. (b) At check-in, the app decodes the QR code locally, validates it against the cached roster, and stores the attendance record (program, youth, scanning official, device, and timestamp) in an encrypted on-device database. (c) Duplicates are checked on the device against records already stored, and again at synchronization, where the server rejects a second record for the same program and youth; if two devices scan the same youth, the earliest timestamp is kept and the later record is marked as a duplicate. (d) Records are queued and uploaded automatically when a connection returns, each with a unique record identifier so retries do not create duplicates, and the official can see each record's status (pending, synced, or rejected). (e) Local data is accessible only after official login and removed after confirmed synchronization, in line with RA~10173. A PWA needs one codebase and no app-store distribution, while a standalone app gives more reliable camera control and offline storage but needs separate maintenance; the choice should be made with the SK Federation and the City ICT Office.
    \item Adoption of enhanced data protection protocols and tested disaster recovery procedures, beyond the platform's built-in backups.
    \item Deep integration with Naga City government digital services and, where legally permitted, national government databases (such as DILG, PSA, and NYC).
    \item Deployment of advanced predictive analytics and reporting modules for long-term budget forecasting and program outcome evaluation.
\end{itemize}
